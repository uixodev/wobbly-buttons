How to choose the perfect domain

Your domain is your website’s address. It’s worth a few minutes of thought, because people will type it, say it and share it for years.

Keep it short and easy to say

The best addresses are easy to say out loud. If you have to spell it every time you tell someone, it’s too complicated. Say it to a friend: if they can type it correctly without help, you’re on the right track.

Use your name, or something close to it

For a person or a small business, your name or business name is usually the best choice, because it’s what people will search for. If it’s taken, add a simple word that fits (such as the city or what you do), rather than strange spellings.

Avoid the awkward bits

  • Hyphens and numbers. They’re easy to forget and hard to say.
  • Words that run together badly. Read the full address once to make sure it doesn’t say something unintended.
  • Names too close to another business. It confuses customers and can cause trouble later.

Pick the ending

.com is the one people try first. For a business in the UK, .co.uk is also common and trusted. If your first choice is taken with one ending, think twice before taking a different ending of the same name, because visitors might end up on the wrong site.

Check it works everywhere

Before you buy it, see whether the same name is free on the social media accounts you plan to use, so you look consistent everywhere.

Buy it in your own name

A domain is rented yearly and renews automatically if you let it. Make sure it’s registered to you, not to someone else, and keep the login details safe. You should always be the owner of your own address.
