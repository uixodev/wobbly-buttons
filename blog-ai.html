How I use AI tools as a web designer

AI is useful in my work, but it is a tool, not the designer. Here is where I use it, where I don’t, and what that means for your website.

The short version

I use AI tools the way I use a sketchbook or a search engine: to get unstuck, to move faster on the boring parts, and to check my own thinking. The look, the personality and the decisions stay with me.

Where it helps

  • First drafts of words. When a page needs a headline or a short description, I ask for a handful of rough options. They’re never the final text, but a bad draft is a quick way to find out what the good one should say.
  • Ideas and directions. Colour moods, layout ideas, names for sections. I treat the answers as prompts for my own thinking, not as answers.
  • Repetitive code. Small helpers, tidy-ups and the dull parts of a build go faster with a second pair of hands. I read and test everything before it goes anywhere near your site.
  • Explaining and debugging. When something behaves oddly, asking “why would this happen?” often gets me to the cause sooner.

Where I keep it out of the way

  • The look and feel. The hand-drawn shapes, the little details and the personality of a site are chosen by hand, one by one. That’s the part you’re hiring me for.
  • Your story. Your words should sound like you, so I write them with you, not for a machine to guess at.
  • The final say. Whatever a tool suggests, I’m the one who checks it, changes it and is responsible for the result.

What that means for your project

More of my time goes into the parts you’ll notice: the design, the details and getting the site to feel like yours. If you’re curious about how I’d use these tools on your project, just ask. I’m happy to explain.
